The idea

You run a one-oven bakery. Every card is a cake with a dial: you turn it every turn, it rises, peaks and then burns. Pull each cake when it is worth the most. Or slam the oven door on a rival's rising cake and wreck their timing. Most points wins, and you can also play it alone.

Your cards

Each card is a cake, and its four edges are the dial. At the start of your turn, give every cake in your oven a quarter-turn clockwise. The edge now on top is the cake's stage:

  • Batter (top edge): nothing to score yet.
  • Risen, Golden, Dark: points. The gold badge shows the peak.
  • One more turn after Dark and it is cinders: the card goes in the Bin and scores nothing.

Setup

  1. Shuffle the 18 cakes and deal 3 face up.
  2. Player 2 picks one for their oven, then Player 1 picks one. Put it in a rack with Batter on top. Each baker has two racks.
  3. The third card starts the Counter. Deal it up to 4 cards. The rest is the deck.
  4. Player 1 goes first.

Days

The game lasts two days. A day ends when the deck and the Counter are both empty.

Washing up: shuffle the Sold pile into a new deck. Everything in the Bin leaves the game. Deal a new Counter. Cakes still in the oven stay there. Player 2 starts day 2. When day 2 ends, cakes still baking score nothing.

Your turn

1. Turn every cake in your oven a quarter-turn clockwise.  2. Do one thing.  3. Refill the Counter to 4 cards.

PullTake a cake that is Risen, Golden or Dark. Score the number on its top edge, do its effect, then put it on the Sold pile.
BakeTake a Counter card into an empty rack, Batter edge up.
SlamTuck a Counter card sideways under an opposing cake that is Batter or Risen. Turn that cake a quarter-turn at once. The card goes in the Bin.
BinTake a Counter card and put it in the Bin, so your rival can't have it.

If the Counter is empty you must pull. If you can do nothing, skip your turn.

Slam the door: how it works

A slam makes the cake skip a stage. It turns once when you slam it, and again when its owner starts their turn. Slam when the next number is the peak, and they lose it.

Their Soufflé is Batter. Its next numbers are 9 (Risen), then 2 (Golden).
You slam it. It jumps to Risen at once. Then on their turn it turns on to Golden.
They score 2, not 9. But slam a cake whose next number is small and the one after is big, and you help them!

Only Batter or Risen cakes can be slammed: once a cake is Golden or Dark it has set.

Card effects

Most cakes have an effect that happens when you pull them. Use a card means: bake it, slam with it, or put it in the Bin.

Winning

After day 2, the baker with the most points wins. A tie is a shared win.

Quick answers

  • Can I pull a Batter cake? No. It must be Risen, Golden or Dark.
  • Can I throw away a cake of my own? No. Pull it, or let it burn.
  • What happens to a Dark cake that gets Draughted? It turns to cinders.
  • Do I turn a cake I just baked? Not on that turn. It starts turning on your next one.

Playing alone

There are two ways to play solo.

Score attack. One oven with two racks, and a Counter of 3. After each turn the oldest Counter card goes cold and leaves the game. No slamming, and ignore Draught. Play two days and grade yourself below.

Rival Baker. Play the two-player game against an automated rival, using the Rival Baker deck. Its rules are on the deck sheet. Casual players beat it about 38 games in 100, and strong players about 69.

Tips for new bakers

  • Pull at the peak. Check the next edge: if it is no bigger than the one showing, pull now.
  • Two cakes peaking together? You can only pull one. Bake so your peaks take turns.
  • A Golden cake is safe from a slam, but a Draught can still hit it, and a slow cake keeps a rack busy for longer.
  • Spend a slam where it hurts. Look for a rising cake whose next number is the biggest.
