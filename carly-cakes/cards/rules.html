Carly Cakes

1–2 bakers25 minutes18 cardsages 10+

The idea

You run a one-oven bakery. Every card is a cake with a dial: you turn it every turn, it rises, peaks and then burns. Pull each cake when it is worth the most. Or slam the oven door on a rival's rising cake and wreck their timing. Most points wins, and you can play alone too.

Your cards

Each card is a cake, and its four edges are the dial. At the start of your turn, give every cake in your oven a quarter-turn clockwise. The edge now on top is the cake's stage:

  • Batter (top edge): nothing to score yet.
  • Risen, Golden, Dark: points. The gold badge shows the peak.
  • One more turn after Dark and it is cinders: the card goes in the Bin and scores nothing.
PinkQuick riser. Peaks at Risen.YellowSteady baker. Peaks at Golden.VioletSlow burn. Peaks at Dark.

The ★ Carly’s tag marks Carly’s own cakes. The rest are classic guests.

Setup

  1. Shuffle the 18 cakes and deal 3 face up.
  2. Player 2 picks one for their oven, then Player 1 picks one. Put it in a rack with Batter on top. Each baker has two racks.
  3. The third card starts the Counter. Deal it up to 4 cards. The rest is the deck.
  4. Player 1 goes first.

Days

The game lasts two days. A day ends when the deck and the Counter are both empty.

Washing up: shuffle the Sold pile into a new deck. Everything in the Bin leaves the game. Deal a new Counter. Cakes still in the oven stay there. Player 2 starts day 2. When day 2 ends, cakes still baking score nothing.

Your turn

1. Turn every cake in your oven a quarter-turn clockwise.  2. Do one thing.  3. Refill the Counter to 4 cards.

PullTake a cake that is Risen, Golden or Dark. Score the number on its top edge, do its effect, then put it on the Sold pile.
BakeTake a Counter card into an empty rack, Batter edge up.
SlamTuck a Counter card sideways under an opposing cake that is Batter or Risen. Turn that cake a quarter-turn at once. The card goes in the Bin.
BinTake a Counter card and put it in the Bin, so your rival can't have it.

If the Counter is empty you must pull. If you can do nothing, skip your turn.

In the box: 18 cake cards (11 of Carly's own, 7 classic guests). For solo play against a rival, add the 6 Rival Baker cards.

Why not bake first?

Because both racks were full, so a Bake was not allowed. When your racks are full, your choices are Pull, Slam or Bin. Pulling the cake that would lose the most by waiting is usually right.

Pavlova reads 2 (Risen), then 5 (Golden). Slam it at Batter and it skips the 2 and goes straight to 5. You have helped your rival! Only slam a cake whose next number is its peak.

What can be slammed?

  • Batter or Risen cakes with a basic slam.
  • Golden and Dark cakes are set. A basic slam can't touch them.
  • Draught can hit any cake at any stage. A Dark cake that gets Draughted turns to cinders.

Winning

After day 2, the baker with the most points wins. A tie is a shared win.

Quick answers

  • Pull a Batter cake? No. It must be Risen, Golden or Dark.
  • Throw away my own cake? No. Pull it, or let it burn.
  • Turn a cake I just baked? Not that turn.

Playing alonePage 4

Score attack

One oven with two racks, and a Counter of 3. After each turn the oldest Counter card goes cold and leaves the game. No slamming, and ignore Draught. Play two days, add up your points and find your grade. A win is Apprentice or better.

Burnt Offering36 or lessLoseThe batch is ruined.
Soggy Bottom37–42LoseIt rose, then it didn't hold.
Apprentice43–49WinYou got it out of the oven.
Journeyman50–56WinCompetent. You'd get hired.
Head Baker57–62WinYou ran the bakery well.
Master Baker63+WinNear-perfect. Rare.

In simulation a casual player earns Apprentice or better about 59 games in 100 and a good player about 79. Master Baker is rare.

Rival Baker

Beat me if you can!

Play the two-player game against an automated rival, using the 6 Rival Baker cards. Flip a card on its turn. Casual players beat it about 38 games in 100, and strong players about 69. A tie goes to the Rival.

The Rival does the first of these that works

1
Pull
a cake that can't get better: next number the same or lower, or Dark.
2
Slam
a Batter or Risen cake if it would lose 3 or more.
3
Bake
the biggest Counter card, if it has a free rack.
4
Bin
the left-most Counter card.

Tips for new bakers

Pull at the peak. Check the next edge. If it is no bigger than the one showing, pull now.
Two cakes peaking together? You can only pull one. Bake so your peaks take turns.
A Golden cake is safe from a slam, but a Draught can still hit it, and a slow cake keeps a rack busy for longer.
Spend a slam where it hurts. Look for a rising cake whose next number is the biggest.
