# Hexside Playtest — Full Match: All Current Canonical Rules (48 Turns)

**Date:** 2026-09-04
**Format:** Full standard match — 24 turns per side, halftime swap, 48 total
**Ruleset:** Full current canon, all now written into the rulebook — Shot +1, tuned Discipline (Standard Challenge pool), the ball-carrier-always-activatable rule (§5), and free kickoff-taker choice with own-half formation freedom (§4/§7).
**Sides:** Both aggressive, as in every full match this project has run.

**Final score: Team A 0 – 1 Team B.**

---

## Headline: zero lockouts, for the first time at full length

**0 cycles in 48 turns (0%).** Not one turn, on either side, across an entire full match, was a forced pass-with-nothing-to-do. That's an improvement even on the already-strong ball-carrier-exception result from earlier today (~2.1%, one accepted three-Counters edge case) — this match didn't hit even that rare deadlock. With the full current ruleset in place, the lockout problem that motivated this whole line of changes (~21.7% baseline aggregate before any of today's fixes) looks fully solved, not just improved.

## The match: five cards, one goal, a wall of a Goalkeeper

Discipline was heavy on both sides — **3 Red Cards** (Team A's Poacher sent off direct in the 5th turn; Team B's Poacher via second Yellow in the first half; Team B's Playmaker sent off direct early in the second half), **3 Yellow Cards**, **5 Clean Challenges**, **3 Warnings**, and **2 Advantage/Play On** calls, from **16 total Tackle attempts**. Both sides spent long stretches down to 4 men, and Team B finished the match with only 3 (Goalkeeper, Sweeper, Wingback).

The one statistically notable thing this match produced: **all 16 Tackle attempts failed** — every single one resolved "defender holds." That's a genuine dice outlier (the Standard Challenge pool and net-success-needed math both make a clean disciplinary record and a run of failed tackles plausible together), not a rule issue — Clean Challenge draws repeatedly saved both sides from further cards despite the constant failed pressure.

Team A's Goalkeeper was the story of the match: cornered twice by Team B's Playmaker in the first half (once in each corner of the defensive third) and forced into contested dribbles to escape both times — winning both. In the second half, with Team B down to 3, it held out through five further direct tackle attempts without conceding once, right through the final whistle.

The only goal came from a genuine mistake under pressure rather than a breakdown: Team A's Goalkeeper, attempting to link the ball forward with a contested pass into a marked Playmaker, lost the roll; the interception handed Team B's Poacher the ball with the goal completely unguarded (Team A had no one home in the box at that moment), for the match's only automatic empty-net goal. From there, the rest of the match — including three further contested shots (all saved) — produced no further score.

## One playtest-process note, not a rule bug

Partway through the second half, I (the person running this playtest, not the ruleset) briefly played Team B's recovered loose ball toward the wrong end for a couple of turns — column A is Team B's own defended goal after the half's end-swap, not their attacking target. I caught it before any shot was attempted at the wrong net and corrected course by turning that possession into a safe pass back to the Goalkeeper instead. No rule or code was at fault; worth flagging for transparency since it affected a few turns of tactical framing in the log, even though it didn't touch the actual rules being tested or the final score.

## Bottom line

This is the cleanest full match this project has run: a completely lockout-free 48 turns, heavy and varied discipline consequences that felt earned rather than swingy, and a 0-1 scoreline built on a real defensive performance rather than a rules artifact. Nothing here suggests any further changes are needed before treating the current rulebook as stable.
