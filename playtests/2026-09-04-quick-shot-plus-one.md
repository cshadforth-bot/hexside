# Hexside Playtest — Shot +1 Experiment (Single Half, 12 Turns/Side)

**Date:** 2026-09-04
**Format:** Single half, 12 turns per side (24 total) — a focused test, not a full match
**Experimental change:** Shot stat +1 across every outfield position (GK unchanged, since keepers don't shoot):
SW 1→2, WB 1→2, PM 2→3, PO 3→4. Save stat unchanged (GK still 3). Everything else — the escape-hex rule, Clearance, Zone/Zone-Pair fallback, failed-Tackle Flair cost, Tactic cycling, Full Press clarification, free Slide Tackle — carried over from the current draft.
**Sides:** Both aggressive, testing the "top 3 reasons games are low-scoring" hypothesis that a Shot-stat buff would fix low conversion.

**Final score: Team A 0 – 0 Team B.**

---

## The actual finding: this test never got to test its own hypothesis

Re-checking the full turn log line by line: **not one Shot was attempted by either side in 24 turns.** Every single attacking sequence — and there were several genuine ones, both sides broke into advanced positions six or seven separate times — was stopped by losing a Dribble Challenge or a Tackle *before* a shot became possible. The Shot+1 buff never got a single roll to prove or disprove itself on.

That's a real result, just not the one the test was built to produce. It's also directly informative for the "why is this low-scoring" question from last time: **reason #1 (compounding contest odds favoring the defense on the way to a chance) is doing so much of the suppressing that reason #2 (Shot vs. Save odds) never even got tested.** If a side can't reliably survive 2-3 sequential Dribble Challenges/Tackles to reach the box in the first place, buffing what happens once they're there is solving the wrong stage of the pipeline.

## A confound I should flag on myself

Part of why no shot happened is a tactical choice I made, not just the dice: I leaned on the **Sweeper** (Shot Range 1) as the primary ball-carrier through most of this match, since it was the unit that kept winning the ball back. A Sweeper with Shot Range 1 has to be standing almost on top of the net to shoot at all — it was never a realistic shooting outlet, and I never successfully relayed the ball on to the Playmaker (Shot Range 2) or Poacher (Shot Range 3, and the one carrying the +1 buff up to 4) before losing it again. A sharper human opponent — or a differently-scripted playtest — would prioritize getting the ball to the long-range shooter before committing it into the final third, rather than dribbling the short-range unit all the way in. So this test is a genuine data point on how hard it is to sustain possession through midfield, but it's an unreliable one on Shot+1 specifically, because the unit best positioned to test the buff (Poacher) barely touched the ball in the second half of the match.

## What did happen

- Multiple real breakthroughs both ways (B reached D3/C4/D4 three separate times in Team A's defensive third; A reached F4/G5 three separate times pushing toward B's goal) — this wasn't a stalemate in the sense of nothing happening, it was a stalemate in the sense of nothing *finishing*.
- The Zone/Zone-Pair fallback and Command Card cycling both worked exactly as designed — cycling fired 6 times in 24 turns (25%), again mostly on Lane/Third cards missing whichever unit had the ball, consistent with what the last full match showed.
- Full Press (once, by A) and the free-Slide-Tackle mechanic (never drawn into a hand again) behaved as expected/unremarkable.
- Tackle attempts and Dribble Challenges traded evenly across the match — neither side dominated the midfield battle, which is why possession kept changing hands but neither could break all the way through to a shot.

## Bottom line and recommendation

This test is **inconclusive on the Shot+1 hypothesis** — it needs a rerun that either (a) plays a longer match so a shot opportunity eventually occurs regardless of carrier choice, or (b) is explicitly scripted to relay the ball to a long-range shooter (Playmaker/Poacher) once the attack reaches the final third, rather than following whichever unit happens to win the ball back. Given zero shots occurred even with the buff live, my read is that reason #1 (surviving the run of contests just to get a look at goal) is the more load-bearing bottleneck of the two — Shot+1 may still help once that hurdle is cleared, but this match didn't clear it often enough to say.
