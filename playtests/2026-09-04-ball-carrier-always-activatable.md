# Hexside Playtest — "Ball-Carrier Always Activatable" Rule Test (24 Turns)

**Date:** 2026-09-04
**Format:** Single half, 12 turns per side (24 total)
**Rule under test (NOT yet written into the rulebook):** Whenever a side holds the ball, its ball-carrying unit is always includable in that turn's activation, regardless of what the played Command Card would otherwise cover — a generalization of the existing kickoff-spot exception (§7) to every turn, not just the restart. Applies only on your own turn with your own ball-carrier; grants nothing on defense.
**Ruleset otherwise:** Current canon — Shot +1, tuned Discipline (Standard Challenge pool).
**Sides:** Both aggressive, as usual.

**Final score: Team A 0 – 0 Team B.**

---

## The headline number

**Zero lockout turns in 24.** Every single turn produced a genuine activation and a real decision — no side was ever forced to cycle (discard-and-redraw) because its hand couldn't reach anything relevant. That's compared against a baseline aggregate of **~21.7%** across the last four comparable playtests (`full2`, `quick3`, `full3`, `full4`), and as high as 33% in the most directly comparable prior 24-turn test (`quick3`). This is the cleanest result any single change has produced this session.

Several turns this match specifically depended on the new rule to produce a real play — cards like `Third-3`, `Third-1`, `ZP-T1-TopCenter`, and even a `Counter` card (which isn't playable on your own turn anyway, but showed the mechanic firing) returned the ball-carrier as an option purely because of the new exception, not because the carrier happened to already be standing in the named zone. Without the rule, several of those turns would have been forced cycles under the old logic.

## What this cost, if anything

Nothing structural. Dribble Challenges, Tackles, and support-unit activation are all exactly as risky as before — the rule only ever guarantees the *one* unit that already has the ball isn't frozen. It doesn't grant an extra activation, doesn't help the defense, and doesn't touch any dice math. The match still had real defensive success: 9 Tackle attempts, a Yellow Card, and a straight Red Card (Team B's Playmaker, sent off turn 18) — Team B finished the half a player down and still had a stretch of good defending afterward (winning a Tackle on turn 22 to reclaim the ball). The rule didn't make possession un-loseable; it just made sure a side already holding the ball always had *something* to do with it.

## The one goal-scoring wrinkle

Only 1 shot was attempted all half (saved, from Team B's Playmaker at C3), which is on the low side for this project's per-half averages. That's very likely just this match's particular dice/hand draw rather than anything caused by the new rule — with a sample of one 24-turn half, this isn't a reliable read on scoring either way. Worth watching in a longer test if this rule moves forward, but it's not the number that mattered for what this test was built to check.

## Bottom line

This is about as clean a "yes, this works" result as a single-variable playtest can produce. The lockout problem you flagged, quantified at ~22% of turns in recent play, dropped to genuinely zero in this test, without any visible cost to the game's defensive tension. Recommend writing it into §5/§7 as proposed — folding the existing kickoff-specific carve-out into the new general rule — and then running a full 48-turn match to confirm the scoring rate holds up over a longer sample.
