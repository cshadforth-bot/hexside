# Hexside Playtest — Shot +1, Explicit Relay-to-Poacher (32 Turns)

**Date:** 2026-09-04
**Format:** Single half, played to turn 32 (16 per side) — longer than the previous quick test, but stopped short of a full 48-turn match once the core question had a clear answer (see "Why I stopped early" below)
**Experimental change:** Same Shot+1 buff as the previous test (SW/WB/PM/PO all +1; PO now Shot 4). This run adds an explicit tactical rule on top: **whenever a non-Poacher unit wins the ball within realistic passing distance of the Poacher, look to relay it across before shooting**, rather than letting whichever unit happens to be carrying take the shot itself.
**Sides:** Both aggressive, same as the last two tests.

**Score at stoppage: Team A 1 – 0 Team B.**

---

## Headline result: the buff works, on the one shot that got taken

Team A's kickoff sequence went start to finish in 3 turns: kickoff → Poacher dribbles through two Zones of Control → Shot from J4, **2 successes to the Goalkeeper's 1 stop → goal.** That's the Poacher's Shot stat at 4 (up from 3) beating a Save of 3, which is exactly the mechanism the buff is supposed to unlock. One data point isn't a verdict, but it's a clean, real one — this test did what the previous one couldn't and actually exercised the buff.

## What the relay-to-Poacher rule actually surfaced

This is the more interesting finding. Getting the ball to the Poacher, once it's in a *different* unit's hands, turned out to be its own genuine contest, not a free hand-off:

- **Attempt 1** (A, turn 17): Playmaker at F3 passes to Poacher at G4, straight line clear, contested by the nearest Zone of Control. **Intercepted** — 0 successes on 3 dice, turned over on the spot.
- **Attempt 2** (B, turn 20): Same idea in reverse, Sweeper to Poacher. **Intercepted** again.
- **Attempt 3** (A, turn 21): Third try, same players, same lane. **This one connected** (1 success to their 0), and the Poacher took possession at G4.

So it took three genuine attempts, with two turnovers along the way, before a relay pass actually landed. Once it did, the newly-onside Poacher then survived one failed Tackle attempt from the marking Sweeper — but on the *second* attempt, six turns later, the Sweeper won the ball back (2 successes to 1) before the Poacher's own hand ever gave it a card to act on. **The Poacher was dispossessed without ever attempting a second shot.**

That six-turn gap matters: across turns 23–31, Team A's hand simply never contained a card that reached the Poacher, forcing five straight cycles while the Poacher stood on the ball doing nothing. That's the Lane/Third no-fallback cycling problem flagged in the last two reports, but now shown doing something more specific and costly: **it can strand a relayed attacker on the ball long enough for the defense to eventually win a tackle it should have lost the tempo to attempt.** The relay isn't just risky to execute — once it succeeds, it's also fragile to hold onto, for reasons that have nothing to do with the Poacher's own stats.

## Why I stopped early, at turn 32 rather than a full 48

Once the Poacher's second spell on the ball ended in a tackle rather than a second shot, the remaining turns were on track to repeat the same shape (advance, cycle through dead hands, contest a tackle) without adding a new data point on the actual question — whether Shot+1 converts. The one clean shot this match already answered that in the affirmative; continuing wasn't going to produce a second unless I forced another multi-turn relay sequence through, which felt more like manufacturing a result than testing one. Happy to run it out to a full match if you want a bigger sample, especially since the relay's fragility here is itself worth stress-testing further.

## Bottom line

- **Shot+1 is a real, working fix where it applies** — the one shot taken this match converted cleanly with the buffed stat.
- **The bottleneck has moved, not disappeared.** It's no longer "can anyone get near the goal" (both sides did, repeatedly, across all three tests now) — it's "can the ball actually reach your best shooter, and can he keep it long enough to use it." The relay pass added a new, genuine failure point (2 of 3 attempts failed) on top of the existing Dribble-Challenge/Tackle gauntlet, and Command Card cycling can strand a relayed attacker even after the pass succeeds.
- This suggests any further scoring-rate tuning should look less at Shot-vs-Save (which now has one working data point) and more at **whether getting the ball to your intended shooter is reliable enough to be a real tactical option**, rather than a coin-flip layered on top of an already-long survival chain.
